A photoreal Stonehenge that is also a working astronomical calendar.

The sun in Henge is not an animation. It is computed — the same ephemeris astronomers use, accurate across five thousand years — and the stones cast exactly the shadows that sun demands. Stand at the Altar Stone on midsummer morning and watch the sunrise arrive over the Heel Stone, within a solar diameter of where the builders aligned it forty-five centuries ago.

THE MONUMENT
• The whole circle raised: thirty sarsen uprights and their lintel ring, the five great trilithons, the bluestone circle and horseshoe, the Altar, Slaughter and Heel Stones, the Station Stone rectangle and the fifty-six Aubrey holes — every stone carrying its Petrie number.
• On real ground: the terrain is Salisbury Plain's own survey data, and the ridge the midsummer sun clears is the one that actually stands there.
• Weathered sarsen and Preseli bluestone, damp courses and lichen, wind moving through the grass, torchlight for the dark hours.

THE CALENDAR
• The Wheel of the Year: solstices, equinoxes and the four cross-quarter festivals — Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasadh, Samhain — each landing on the instant the sun actually arrives, not on a calendar date.
• Moon phases, eclipse seasons and the 18.6-year lunar standstill, solved from the real ephemeris.
• A time machine from 3000 BC to AD 3000. Watch Thuban hold the celestial pole for the builders in an age when Polaris was no use to anyone.
• The sky over your own longitude, or over Wiltshire.
