# Lunar markers at Stonehenge: Hawkins, Hoyle, and what the evidence will bear

**Status:** synthesis of four audited research strands (2026-07-28). Auditor verdicts folded in; corrections noted inline.

## Honesty preamble

A "moving markers" mode is a **visualization of two modern hypotheses** published in the 1960s — Gerald Hawkins's and Fred Hoyle's Aubrey-hole eclipse predictors — not a reconstruction of anything the builders are known to have done. No excavated object from Stonehenge has ever been identified as a movable marker; every marker scheme postulates perishable or removable tokens precisely because nothing survives to test (Atkinson 1966; Ruggles 1999). The attested contents of the Aubrey Holes are cremated human remains and sockets for *fixed* uprights. Movability is a property of the models, not of the archaeological record. The mode must say so on screen, every time.

## The schemes (Hawkins vs Hoyle mechanics, precisely)

Both schemes exploit one arithmetic coincidence: the lunar nodes regress around the ecliptic in 18.61 years, and 3 × 18.61 = 55.83 ≈ 56 — the number of Aubrey Holes — an error of ~0.3% (about one hole of drift per ~30 years for a 3-holes-per-year node marker).

### Hawkins: an annual-tick eclipse-year predictor

Hawkins ("Stonehenge: A Neolithic Computer", *Nature* 202, 1258–1261, 1964; *Stonehenge Decoded*, 1965) proposed:

- **Six marker stones**, three white and three black alternating, placed around the 56-hole ring at intervals of **9, 9, 10, 9, 9, 10** holes.
- **Each marker advances one hole per year**, all in the same direction (the scheme works clockwise or anticlockwise so long as it is consistent).
- The arithmetic: winters in which the midwinter full moon rises over the Heel Stone — the geometry that puts the moon near a node at solstice syzygy, the eclipse-danger condition — recur at intervals of **19, 19, 18 years** (sum 56). Six markers at the half-intervals (9, 9, 10) ensure a marker of the right colour reaches each reference hole on schedule; the whole configuration repeats exactly every 56 years.
- The read-out is annual and categorical: a marker arriving at the key hole on the Heel Stone axis flags an eclipse **"danger year"** — eclipses of sun or moon can occur near the solstices (and, via other reference holes, the equinoxes). It predicts eclipse *years/seasons*, not dates and not visibility from Britain.
- Hawkins proposed the **30 sarsen archways** as the day-counter for the 29.53-day synodic month (with narrow Stone 11 making the count effectively alternate 29/30), reaching the day once the Aubrey ring flagged the year. (The archway claim is in the 1964 abstract; the Stone-11 detail is standard in accounts of the book but was not verified against the primary text — treat as Debated.)

*Sourcing caveat (auditor):* the specific detail that **holes 56, 51 and 5** were the fixed reference positions (white marker at AH56 = midwinter full moon over the Heel Stone) rests, in this research pass, on a single hobbyist website (tivas.org.uk) and was not checked against the 1965 book. It is plausible as a summary of Hawkins but should be treated as **unverified-secondary** until someone reads *Stonehenge Decoded* directly. The same applies to the 9,9,10 spacing and one-hole-per-year rule generally: consistent across multiple independent secondary accounts, but no strand read the 1965 primary. Do **not** source Hawkins's mechanics from Wikipedia's "Aubrey holes" article, whose summary garbles them (it assigns Hoyle-style daily moves to Hawkins). Before any of this enters app prose, pin the mechanics to Hawkins 1964/1965 by direct reading.

### Hoyle: a daily-tick orrery of the ecliptic

Hoyle ("Stonehenge — an Eclipse Predictor", *Nature* 211, 454–456, 1966; "Speculations on Stonehenge", *Antiquity* 40 (160), 262–276, 1966; *On Stonehenge*, W.H. Freeman, 1977) treated the Aubrey circle as a 56-point model of the ecliptic, each hole 1/56 of the circle. **Four markers:**

| Marker | Rule | Circuit | Approximates |
|---|---|---|---|
| M (moon) | +2 holes per day | 28 days | 27.32-day sidereal month |
| S (sun) | +2 holes every 13 days | 364 days | tropical year |
| N, N′ (nodes, kept diametrically opposite) | −3 holes per year (**retrograde**, opposite direction to S and M) | 18.67 yr | 18.61-yr nodal regression |

- **Phase read-out:** S and M in the same hole = new moon; diametrically opposite = full moon. Phase is the S–M angle — the moon marker alone tracks ecliptic position, not phase.
- **Eclipse rule:** solar eclipse possible when S and M coincide at (or within about one hole of) a node marker; lunar eclipse possible when S sits at one node and M at the opposite node at full moon. The device predicts eclipse *seasons* (node passages every ~173.3 days) — that an eclipse is possible somewhere on Earth — never local visibility.
- **Recalibration is mandatory**, because the integer rates drift (sun marker ~1.25 days/yr, moon marker ~0.7 day/month; 2 holes/day overruns the sidereal month by ~0.7 d and undershoots the synodic by ~1.5 d): the sun marker is reset twice yearly at the solstices against the monument's axis; the moon marker is reset at each full moon by placing it opposite the sun marker; the node markers are calibrated from the extreme moonrise/moonset azimuths along the station-stone sightlines (and corrected whenever an eclipse is actually observed). The scheme only works as a continually recalibrated analogue model, never as a free-running clock.

**Claimed vs possible.** Hawkins's careful *Nature* phrasing is that the holes "can be used" this way; the book argues it was the monument's purpose while conceding it is unprovable. Hoyle asserted more: that exactly 56 holes plus the station rectangle evidence deliberate design, implying the builders tracked the invisible, purely inferred lunar node. (The oft-quoted "veritable Newton or Einstein" phrasing attributed to Hoyle was **not verified** in the primary text this pass — do not use it in-app.) For the renderer: Hawkins is an annual-tick automaton tied to the monument's sightline holes; Hoyle is a daily-tick orrery whose marker geometry directly encodes moon phase and eclipse condition. That characterization is our synthesis and carries the tier of its inputs — **Debated**, not Attested (auditor correction).

## The Station Stone lunar lines

The four Station Stones (91 and 93 surviving; 92 and 94 reduced to the "South Barrow" and "North Barrow") stand on the Aubrey circle and form a rectangle of roughly 80 × 33 m.

- **Short sides (91–92, 93–94):** parallel to the solstitial axis, azimuth 49–50°, declination ±24° — midsummer sunrise NE, midwinter sunset SW (Ruggles 1997, Table 7). *Attested geometry.*
- **Long sides (94–91, 93–92):** perpendicular, azimuth ~140°/~320° — SE toward the southernmost moonrise and NW toward the northernmost moonset of the **major lunar standstill** (declination ≈ ±29°, reached only for a year or two every 18.6 years). Ruggles's survey gives 93→92 at azimuth 140°, declination −29°. *Attested geometry; intent Debated.* Priority belongs to C. A. "Peter" Newham (Yorkshire Post, 16 March 1963; *The Enigma of Stonehenge*, 1964 — **not 1966**, an attribution error in one strand corrected by the auditor); Hawkins reached it independently (*Nature* 200, 1963).
- **The latitude coincidence:** only within roughly ±0.5° of latitude of Stonehenge's 51.2°N (Hawkins said ±30 miles) do the extreme solar and lunar directions cross at right angles, allowing a rectangle rather than a parallelogram. The trigonometry is real and checkable; the inference that the builders chose or understood the latitude for this reason is **Debated at best** — Ruggles treats the perpendicularity as a coincidence that may or may not have been exploited.
- **The diagonal 91–93** (Hawkins's claimed minor-standstill line, ±19°) is generally **rejected**: Ruggles measured its declination at −16°, "no obvious explanation in terms of the sun or moon", and the sightline crosses the obstructed centre.
- Ruggles calls the rectangle the *single most convincing* lunar element at Stonehenge — while framing the live question as whether the right-angle coincidence "simply passed unnoticed, or… was actively exploited." A 2024–25 English Heritage / Oxford / Leicester / Bournemouth / RAS project (Chadburn, Ruggles, Silva) observed the actual standstill moonrises against the rectangle; English Heritage's own framing: "there is much debate as to whether this was deliberate." Formal results were not located this pass. Informal BAA forum reports of a ~1.5–2° miss on the SE side are **anecdote — keep out of app prose** (auditor: dubious-source, honestly tiered speculative).
- Robin Heath's 5:12-proportion numerology: **Speculative/fringe**; the measured diagonal declination does not match its claimed targets. Exclude from the app.

**Newham's causeway-posthole tally** (*Nature* 211, 456–458, 1966; *The Astronomical Significance of Stonehenge*, 1972) — six rows of postholes on the NE causeway read as stakes planted each winter where the full moon nearest midwinter rose, accumulating ~6 nodal cycles (~112 years) to fix the northern major limit — is a genuine published marker-tally proposal, but Ruggles (1997, 214–17, citing Heggie 1981, 202) judges it **untenable**: the postholes do not cluster toward the limit end as the scheme predicts, wooden posts cannot stand as a century-long record, and the moon near new is invisible when rising in the NE at midsummer. Current archaeology (Cleal et al. 1995) reads the posts as access-restricting timber structures. What survives is looser: the Phase 1 entrance axis (declination +27°) and several southern features (C44 postholes at −28°, the SE cremation cluster near Aubrey Hole 14, bluestone 33e at −27°) all fall **2–3° inside** the moon's major limits — Ruggles's "tentative working hypothesis" of lunar *symbolism* at Stonehenge 1–2, followed by a solar re-dedication in Phase 3. The Heel Stone's secure role is **solar** (with vanished companion 97 it bracketed midsummer sunrise); Burl's lunar-first reading of it is Debated.

## What the Aubrey holes actually held

- **The ring:** 56 pits just inside the bank, dug ~3000 BC; excavated examples average ~1.06 m across and 0.76 m deep (Cleal, Walker & Montague 1995). *Attested.*
- **Cremation cemetery:** Hawley's 1920s excavations found cremated remains in or beside many holes; the remains were reburied in Aubrey Hole 7 in 1935 and re-excavated in 2008 by the Stonehenge Riverside Project. Radiocarbon and Bayesian modelling show cremation deposits spanning ~5 centuries, c. 3000–2500 BC (Willis et al., "The dead of Stonehenge", *Antiquity* 90, 337–356, 2016). *Attested.* On the numbers, per the auditor: the **demonstrated MNI from AH7 is 27** (22 adults + 5 sub-adults); "52–58 individuals" is an *estimate* for the reburied bone from holes, ditch and bank — do not present ~58 as a demonstrated minimum. Strontium isotopes show some individuals were non-local, consistent with west Wales origins (Snoeck et al. 2018, *Scientific Reports*).
- **Posts or bluestones — genuinely contested:** the 1995 English Heritage synthesis concluded the holes held timber posts, later removed; Parker Pearson, Pitts and Richards (2009, and since) argue for small bluestone uprights on pit-morphology and basal-compression grounds, bound up with the contested Waun Mawn hypothesis; Darvill and Pitts maintain the timber (or empty) reading. **Debated** — and either way a ring of *fixed* uprights. No current specialist interprets the 56 holes as tally positions; the count is variously explained geometrically, and the burial function is the one firmly attested use.

## The critique and where consensus sits

- **Atkinson, "Moonshine on Stonehenge" (*Antiquity* 40 (159), 212–216, 1966)** — verified from the open-access PDF: Hawkins used probable natural depressions as sightline points, tolerances up to ~2°, selective posthole inclusion; recomputed against the honest population of 182+ candidate sight-lines, the chance probability of the observed alignments is **0.46–0.97** — fully consistent with accident. The famous verdict on *Stonehenge Decoded*: "tendentious, arrogant, slipshod and unconvincing." His argument that a genuinely useful 56 should recur at other monuments (it does not) is authentically his, though the auditor could not confirm it in the 1966 text — it may belong to his 1982 chapter in Heggie (ed.), *Archaeoastronomy in the Old World*; cite both.
- **Colton & Martin (*Nature* 213, 1967)** and **Heggie (*Megalithic Science*, 1981)** pressed the drift-and-recalibration point and the conceptual one: the lunar node is not an observable object, and the schemes presuppose modern abstractions and unevidenced numeracy.
- **Jacquetta Hawkes, "God in the machine" (*Antiquity* 41 (163), 174–180, 1967** — note Ruggles's bibliography misdates it 1966): the computer reading is the 1960s projecting itself — "every age has the Stonehenge it deserves — or desires."
- **Atkinson's mature verdict (1982):** "only the alignment of the Avenue on the summer solstice sunrise… can be accepted with confidence." **Ruggles (1997)** judged that still "true in broad terms", and stated the standard position verbatim: "there is no reason whatsoever to suppose that at any stage the site functioned as an astronomical observatory — at least in any sense that would be meaningful to a modern astronomer." Equally, Ruggles warns against "astronomical overload" backlash: the Phase 3 solstitial axis is deliberate (good to roughly a degree — the site's horizon and data quality support no better), and rough lunar symbolism in the early phases is a live, tentative hypothesis under active field test (2024–25 standstill project; Ruggles & Chadburn, *Stonehenge: Sighting the Sun*, 2024).
- **The pattern repeats:** Darvill's 2022 sarsen-circle perpetual-calendar proposal (*Antiquity* 96) was rebutted by Magli & Belmonte (*Antiquity* 97, 2023) as numerology — the modern rerun of Hawkins/Atkinson. Consensus after sixty years: **solstitial symbolism yes; possible lunar symbolism early; working calendar or eclipse instrument, no.** Both marker schemes are ingenious demonstrations of what the ring *could* do, not archaeology of what it *did*.

## Design guidance for a "moving markers" mode

**Which markers.** Ship both schemes as explicitly-labelled hypothesis overlays, selectable and never on by default:

1. **Hawkins overlay** — six small stones (3 white, 3 black) on the Aubrey ring at 9,9,10,9,9,10 spacing; tick +1 hole per simulated year; highlight the Heel-Stone-axis hole and flash a "danger year" state when a marker lands there. Do *not* label specific holes 56/51/5 until the 1965 text is verified (see sourcing caveat above); render the axis hole geometrically instead.
2. **Hoyle overlay** — four markers (S, M, N, N′) with rules M +2 holes/day, S +2 holes/13 days, N/N′ −3 holes/year retrograde, N′ locked opposite N. Render phase as the S–M angle; render the eclipse condition (syzygy within ~1 hole of a node) as a distinct state. Hoyle's is the scheme that animates meaningfully at the app's sub-year timescales and can be driven directly from `HengeAstro`'s real sun/moon/node longitudes — offer both "idealized rules with resets" and "true ephemeris positions" so the drift *between* them is itself visible. That drift is the honest pedagogy: it shows why the machine needs its solstice and full-moon recalibrations.

**Movement rules.** All rates as tabulated above; implement Hoyle's resets (sun at solstices against the axis, moon opposite sun at full moon) as visible events, not silent corrections. Compute everything from `HengeAstro` — no hardcoded 18.61 or 29.53 outside test fixtures, per repo rules.

**Station Stone layer.** Draw the four station positions, long-side arrows to SE major-standstill moonrise / NW moonset, short sides sharing the solstice axis, with the latitude-perpendicularity note. Omit the 91–93 diagonal (rejected) and Newham's stake-tally narrative (untenable); the causeway postholes, if shown, are drawn as the moonrise arc they span, labelled as such.

**Tier badging.**

- **Attested:** the 56-hole count, pit dimensions, cremation cemetery (~3000–2500 BC), the rectangle's *geometry*, the solstitial axis, the arithmetic 3 × 18.61 ≈ 56, the existence and content of the 1960s papers and their rebuttals.
- **Debated:** that anything (posts vs bluestones) stood in the Aubrey Holes; that the station-rectangle lunar lines were *intentional*; Ruggles's early-lunar-symbolism hypothesis; Burl's lunar Heel Stone; both marker schemes *as claims about the builders* — and the entire moving-markers animation itself carries this badge.
- **Speculative:** Hawkins's Y/Z-hole and bluestone-count numerology; Heath's 5:12 geometry; any sub-degree precision claim; the BAA 2024 miss anecdote (prefer omitting these entirely).
- **Never present:** the "Newton or Einstein" quote (unverified), Wikipedia-derived mechanics, marker movability as an archaeological fact.

**Suggested in-app wording** (mode intro card):

> *"Hypothesis mode — Debated. In 1964 the astronomer Gerald Hawkins proposed that the 56 Aubrey Holes could be used as an eclipse predictor: six stones advanced one hole each year (Hawkins, Nature 202, 1964; Stonehenge Decoded, 1965). Fred Hoyle refined the idea into a working model of the sky with sun, moon and node markers (Hoyle, Nature 211, 1966). What you are watching is their 1960s arithmetic, animated — not a reconstruction. Archaeologists rejected the claim that the builders did this (Atkinson, Antiquity 40, 1966; Ruggles, Proc. British Academy 92, 1997): the holes' attested use is as a cremation cemetery of about 3000–2500 BC (Willis et al., Antiquity 90, 2016), and no movable marker has ever been found."*

Station-stone caption:

> *"The long sides of the Station Stone rectangle point toward the moon's extreme rising and setting positions, reached every 18.6 years — a geometry that only works as a rectangle near this latitude. Whether the builders intended this is debated (Newham 1963; Hawkins, Nature 200, 1963; Ruggles, Proc. British Academy 92, 1997; English Heritage major-standstill observations, 2024–25)."*
