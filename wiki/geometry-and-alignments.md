# Geometry and alignments

## Surveyed geometry — *Established*

The figures the app computes with, as encoded in
`HengeGeometry/Monument.swift` (Cleal et al. 1995; Petrie's and later
surveys):

| Feature | Figure |
|---|---|
| Monument axis | azimuth **49.9°** (NE, to the midsummer sunrise quarter) |
| Sarsen circle | **33.0 m** diameter, 30 uprights |
| Aubrey ring | **87.0 m** diameter, 56 holes |
| Enclosure earthwork | ~**110 m** across (ditch centreline) |
| Heel Stone | **77 m** out along the axis, 4.7 m tall |
| Avenue | **22 m** wide, straight for ~500 m before turning |
| Station Stones | four, on the Aubrey circle, forming a rectangle |

- The **station rectangle**'s short sides parallel the main axis; its
  long sides run toward the major standstill moonrise/moonset quarters —
  this much is geometry anyone can redo from the plan. Whether it was
  *designed* for the moon is Debated (see the lunar page).
- The **axis alignment** itself — midsummer sunrise up the Avenue,
  midwinter sunset the other way — is Established: it is the plan, not a
  theory about the plan.
- Cardinal directions: the app's overlay draws true north from the
  ephemeris frame, not magnetic.

## The stonehengeology.com constructions — *Speculative*

Source: stonehengeology.com/stonehenge-geometry (fetched 2026-07-31, at
the owner's direction). One author's geometric reconstruction in the
tradition of Alexander Thom's "Megalithic Yard" (MY); enthusiast work
citing real surveys (John Wood 1740, Petrie 1880, Gowland 1901, Hawley
and Atkinson) but presenting derived constructions as demonstrated fact,
without peer review. Every claim below is **Speculative** and must be
badged wherever the app shows it.

Claims, as made:

- Sarsen circle diameter = **36 MY**; 30 stones at 12° spacing with 4°
  gaps; primary axis azimuth 50°.
- An outer **bluestone oval** "dedicated to the moon": two 27-MY-diameter
  circles with centres 2 MY apart (envelope 27 × 29 MY, sum 56 — read as
  the 56 Aubrey holes and a "56-year moon cycle"), its axis offset 24
  megalithic inches from the sarsen centre, aligned on the southernmost
  moonrise / northernmost moonset.
- An inner **bluestone oval** "dedicated to the solstice": 14.5 × 18 MY,
  built from four near-Pythagorean triangles (3.5 : 7.25 : 8 MY), on a
  secondary axis through trilithons 3 and 4.
- A central **3:4:5 triangle** (18:24:30 megalithic inches); the Altar
  Stone 6 MY long, one sixth of the sarsen diameter; the station
  rectangle "1,920 times" the core triangle's volume.
- Aubrey holes 56 and 28 said to have held timber posts fixing the axis
  five centuries before the sarsens.

What survives contact with the established register: the axis azimuth
(50° against the surveyed 49.9°), the stone count, the 56, and the
station rectangle's lunar bearing — all of which the app already carries
from better sources. The Megalithic Yard itself is rejected by
mainstream archaeometry (Thom's unit does not survive statistical
re-analysis); the ovals and triangle constructions are the author's own.

In the app: the geometry overlay draws the established table above with
labels and ground-mapped measurements; the speculative constructions, if
shown, ride the same hypothesis badging as the Hoyle markers.

## The lunar hypotheses — *Debated*

See `research/lunar-markers.md`: Hawkins (1963/1965) and Hoyle (1966) on
the Aubrey ring as an eclipse predictor. The app's gold-marker mode acts
out Hoyle's machine and says so on screen, every time, with the honest
scorecard (42 caught, 0 missed, 9 false alarms over a test decade).
Hawkins's specific mechanics still need primary-source pinning before
any in-app prose uses them.
