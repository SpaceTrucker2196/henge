# The stones and their journeys

## The sarsens — *Established*

- About 30 uprights of the outer circle with a continuous lintel ring;
  five trilithons in the inner horseshoe, graded toward the Great
  Trilithon. Silcrete ("sarsen"), dressed hard on the inward and
  processional faces.
- Provenance: **West Woods, Marlborough Downs, ~25 km north** — Nash et
  al. 2020, *Science Advances* 6:eabc0133, geochemical match of 50 of the
  52 surviving sarsens against the core drilled from Stone 58.
- Deeper petrology, weathering behaviour and lichen cover are worked
  through in `research/sarsen-geology.md` — audited, tiered, and
  deliberately **not yet applied** to the renderer, per the owner.

## The bluestones — *Established*

- The smaller stones of the circle and horseshoe: dolerites, rhyolites
  and tuffs from the **Preseli Hills, west Wales, ~250 km away**;
  specific outcrops identified at Carn Goedog and Craig Rhos-y-felin
  (Bevins, Ixer and colleagues, 2010s), with excavated Neolithic
  quarrying traces (Parker Pearson et al.).

## The Altar Stone came from Scotland — *Established, 2024*

Primary: Clarke et al., "A Scottish provenance for the Altar Stone of
Stonehenge", *Nature* 632, 570–575 (14 August 2024),
doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07652-1. News summary: Gaind & Smith, *Nature*
632, 484–485 (2024), doi:10.1038/d41586-024-02584-2 — the article behind
the owner's reference photograph.

- The Altar Stone — the six-tonne greenish sandstone slab lying at the
  monument's heart — is **not** from Wales as long assumed. U–Pb and
  Lu–Hf dating of its detrital zircon, apatite and rutile grains
  fingerprints the **Orcadian Basin of north-east Scotland**, at least
  **750 km** away (the news piece says "hauled 800 kilometres").
- Implication carried by the paper's authors: long-range Neolithic
  transport — likely by sea — and a degree of connectivity across
  Britain not previously credited for c. 2500 BC.
- In the app the Altar Stone lies recumbent at the heart of the setting;
  its lore note should carry this finding and its citation.

## Construction sequence — *Established, coarse*

- c. 2900 BC: earthwork enclosure and (probably) the Aubrey ring.
- c. 2600–2400 BC: the sarsen circle and trilithons; bluestones
  rearranged repeatedly over the following centuries.
- The app's two states are deliberate poles: **as it was** (the complete
  sarsen monument, c. 2200 BC) and **as it stands** (the surveyed ruin).
  Everything between is real archaeology the app does not yet attempt.

## Medieval reception — *Established as historiography*

From the Nature news piece: Henry of Huntingdon (twelfth century) counted
it among the wonders of England; legend gave the building to Merlin. The
lore panel may quote this as historiography, never as history.

## A popular infographic — *cited, not reproduced*

Source: "Infographics of the construction process of the megalithic
monument of cromlech at Stonehenge (England)", Alamy stock image
2NEC3YA (contributor-owned, commercially licensed; fetched as a PDF
listing 2026-07-31, at the owner's direction). **The artwork itself is
copyrighted stock material and is not vendored or reproduced anywhere
in this repo** — only its factual claims are recorded here, cited to
their source, the way any secondary reference is treated.

What it shows, general-audience quality, English-as-a-second-language
phrasing throughout — treat as **Speculative/Debated** unless a claim
is independently corroborated:

- Etymology panel: "megalithic" from Greek *megas* (big) + *lithos*
  (stone); "cromlech" as stone + circular monument — **Established**,
  ordinary etymology.
- First-phase panel: circular ditch, two original accesses, **110 m
  diameter**, livestock bone deposits in the ditch fill, only one
  causeway still open today — all **corroborated** by our own
  established figure (Cleal et al., see
  [the earthwork page](earthwork-and-ground.md)) and by
  `Earthwork.swift`'s modelled single fully-open axis causeway.
- Third-phase panel: the central structure "surrounded by two rings
  made of holes, consisting of two horseshoes and two circles of
  megalithic [stones]" — matches our sarsen/bluestone circle-and-
  horseshoe model. Labels the Altar Stone and a stone it calls the
  **"Stone of the sacrifice."**
- Labels **"Hole Y"** and **"Hole Z"** on a ring outside the sarsen
  circle — these correspond to the real **Y and Z holes**, a genuine
  excavated ring of pits from a much later phase (c. 1600 BC), and are
  **not yet modelled** in this app. A legitimate gap to add, separate
  from the two claims below.

Two claims from this source are flagged, not carried into the app:

- **"166" as the stone count of the original construction.** This
  number does not match any figure in our established sources (the
  sarsen count, bluestone count, or any published total we've found)
  and is not corroborated elsewhere. Recorded here as what the source
  says; not repeated as fact.
- **"Stone of the sacrifice."** Almost certainly this source's gloss on
  the **Slaughter Stone** — the fallen sarsen at the north-east
  entrance, a name coined by antiquarian observers (its surface iron
  staining was once read as blood) with **no excavated evidence of
  sacrifice** ever performed there. `Scene.swift`'s own comment already
  says as much: "nothing was slaughtered on it." If this label is ever
  shown in-app, it needs the correction attached, not the euphemism
  alone.
