Subject: Two requests on Stonehenge stone data: reuse of RR 32/2012 Appendix 1, and the laser-scan CAD model

Dear Archive Services,

I am the author of Henge, a native iOS and macOS application that renders
Stonehenge as a working astronomical calendar. Sun, moon and star positions
are computed from published theory, the terrain is the Environment Agency's
LiDAR under the Open Government Licence, and every claim about the monument
carries a citation in the app. The remaining gap is the stones themselves,
and I would like to close it with Historic England's data rather than with
guesswork. Two requests, either of which can be answered on its own.

1. Permission to reuse Appendix 1 of Research Report 32/2012

Abbott and Anderson-Whymark, Stonehenge Laser Scan: Archaeological Analysis
(RR 32/2012), Appendix 1, Tables 1 and 2, gives the above-ground surface
area, volume and estimated weight of 74 stones. I would like to vendor those
two tables as a data file inside the app, cite the report by page in the
app's information view and in the repository's data-provenance register,
and use the figures for one purpose: to derive a mean cross-sectional area
per stone (volume over height) so that each stone is drawn at something
close to its real section rather than a shared constant.

The app is sold on the App Store, so this is commercial use. The PDF is
marked © English Heritage without an open licence, and your re-use terms
ask for written permission before commercial exploitation; this is that
request. I would be glad to carry any attribution wording you prefer, and
to send you the derived table for checking before it ships. If the report
is in fact already released under the Open Government Licence, a line
saying so would settle it.

2. Access to the per-stone CAD model from the 2011 laser scan

Bryan, Abbott and Dodson (ISPRS Archives XL-5/W2, 2013) list the 2011
Greenhatch scan deliverables held at Swindon, and I understand a
157-stone solid model derived from it has been shared with researchers:
Trevor Cox at Salford built his acoustic scale model from it (2019), and
Cox, Fazenda and Greaney (Journal of Archaeological Science 122, 2020)
ran their bluestone statistics on the same model. I would like to request
access on the same footing.

What I would do with it: measure per-stone width and thickness at ground
level, which no published table gives, and place each stone by its own
outline. What I would not do: redistribute the model, or any mesh derived
from it. Only the measurements (a table of numbers per Petrie stone
number) would enter the app, cited to Historic England as the source, and
I will accept whatever terms you attach to that table. If a research
agreement or a fee schedule applies, please send it.

Interim state, so you know where the app stands today: positions come
from a published digitisation (CC BY-SA) of the 1989/90 Rees survey sheet
MP/STO0861, credited to Rees and to the Historic England Archive; heights
are taken from Cleal, Walker and Montague 1995, Appendix 5, via the
Archaeology Data Service under its terms of use; and every stone carries
a label saying whether it is surveyed, provisional, or a reconstruction.

Thank you for considering both. I am happy to provide a build of the app,
the repository, or anything else that helps.
